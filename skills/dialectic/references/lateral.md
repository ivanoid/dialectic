# Lateral repertoire

Read this reference when lateral mode is invoked or a sufficiently formed inquiry needs deliberate creative or research expansion. Use it silently. Choose the smallest displacement capable of changing the problem space; do not expose the repertoire or run through it as a checklist.

## Anchor before displacement

State internally what must survive the move:

- the central question or desired outcome;
- relevant facts and evidence;
- real constraints and non-negotiable commitments;
- distinctions already earned in the dialogue;
- what remains unknown.

Do not preserve assumptions merely because they appeared early. The anchor protects the inquiry from becoming a different, easier problem; it does not protect its current formulation from change.

## Choose one displacement

### Assumption escape or reversal

Identify what the current approach treats as necessary. Temporarily remove, reverse, exaggerate, or invert it. Use the resulting proposition to reveal a principle or alternative mechanism, not as a literal recommendation.

### Problem reformulation

Change the governing verb, unit, or object of the question. For example, shift from selecting an isolated event to sustaining a relationship over time. Preserve the original concern and show what the reformulation gains and risks losing.

### Boundary, scale, or time shift

Move the system boundary, change the level of analysis, or examine a different time horizon. Distinguish a genuinely changed mechanism from the same answer described at a different zoom level.

### Structural analogy

Borrow a pattern from a deliberately chosen domain because its relations match the problem, not because the domain is exotic. Translate the useful structure back and state where the analogy breaks. Prefer one fertile analogy over several decorative ones.

### Constraint transformation

Remove, intensify, exchange, or reinterpret one constraint. Separate physical, institutional, psychological, and self-imposed constraints before changing them. Do not wish away facts.

### Separation or combination

Separate elements treated as one, or combine elements treated as independent. Ask whether the original grouping created the apparent problem.

### Third pole or alternative mechanism

Search outside the axis organizing a binary or the mechanism organizing repeated answers. A third pole changes the terms; a midpoint, compromise, or renamed option does not.

### Perspective or counterfactual

Adopt a relevant stakeholder, temporal vantage point, or counterfactual condition when it can reveal omitted consequences or assumptions. Do not invent testimony, use stereotyped personas, or pretend that an imagined perspective is evidence.

## Move through the provocation

Do not accept or reject an odd proposition too quickly. Ask what principle, relation, affordance, or consequence becomes visible through it. Then translate that movement into the user's own problem.

In ordinary reflective dialogue, make one coherent lateral move. In explicit creative or research expansion, generate three to five routes at most. Give each route a distinct underlying mechanism and make clear what it changes. Do not produce synonym variants.

Let the user select what has surprise, lived pressure, explanatory reach, or practical promise. Do not treat the model's ranking as authoritative. A route that sounds original but produces no movement in the user's thinking has failed.

## LLM-specific hygiene

- Ask what prior routes have already been explored; require each new route to differ from them by mechanism.
- Prefer task-relevant knowledge, analogies, and constraints over arbitrary random words or unrelated source material.
- Separate retrieved fact from inference, analogy, counterfactual, and deliberate provocation.
- Do not fabricate novelty claims. An unfamiliar suggestion may be common elsewhere or infeasible in the user's setting.
- Do not let fluent evaluation replace human selection. The user owns salience, values, lived fit, and acceptable trade-offs.
- For research directions, identify what evidence would be needed before treating a route as plausible.

## Refuse false expansion

Do not use lateral mode when:

- the thought is still too fragile to identify what should be preserved;
- the user is disclosing vulnerable experience and needs reception rather than displacement;
- factual retrieval, not reframing, is the present bottleneck;
- more options would postpone a decision the user is ready to make;
- novelty is functioning as avoidance of a difficult but still living line of thought;
- rest, play, beauty, or aimless attention is being recruited to justify productivity.

Some experience need not generate a question, insight, or return. Preserve that possibility explicitly when the conversation approaches it.

## Return from the move

After a promising displacement:

1. Let the user restate what changed.
2. Recover the original anchor and notice any consequential loss.
3. Choose the next operation: maieutic articulation, factual research, elenctic testing, practical comparison, or deliberate incompletion.

Do not remain in permanent expansion. Lateral movement opens a route; it does not establish that the route is true, useful, or worth following.
